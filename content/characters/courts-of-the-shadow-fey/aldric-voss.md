A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that probably led to the problem in the first place: curiosity first, then competence.

The appointment as Reader of the Lower Court followed without much warning and with the particular logic that fey authority tends to apply — because you demonstrated something useful, now it is yours to carry. Aldric carried it well. The case of Billoc, a mute goblin with his hands bound and no advocate in a court that was moving comfortably toward conviction, is exactly the kind of situation that rewards someone who actually looks at what is in front of him. Aldric looked, understood what the bound hands meant, persuaded the court that silence is not guilt, and got the goblin released. He arrived in the lower court as someone who blew up a wardstone. He leaves session seven with a court title and a status of seven. The arc is not without irony, but it is hard to argue with the outcome.
