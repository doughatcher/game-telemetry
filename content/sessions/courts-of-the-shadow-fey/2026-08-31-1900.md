The lower court of the Shadow Fey palace moved to rhythms the party was only beginning to understand: obligation paid in small coin, reputation built by inches, and every slight or service remembered with the particular exactness of creatures who have been counting slights since before Zobeck had walls. The day that followed their arrival at the palace service level proved that the court could be navigated without a sword drawn, provided one was willing to be patient, clever, and occasionally humble.

The first reckoning fell to Aldric. King Mago's summons arrived without ceremony, and the wizard found himself standing before the goblin king with the memory of his own fireball — and the wardstone it had shattered on the Shadow Road — very much alive in the room. The damaged stone had functioned as a message ward, an alarm woven into the road itself, and Aldric had broken it in the act of dying and coming back. He examined it carefully, said nothing he did not mean, and then repaired it with the attentive precision of a man who understood that this moment was a test of more than his spellcraft. Ten-Pound Hammer, the goblin smith who had inspected the work, accepted the repair without ceremony. King Mago's response was more consequential: he appointed Aldric Reader of the Lower Court, a title that carried weight enough to matter and responsibility enough to be inconvenient.

The inconvenience arrived almost immediately in the shape of Billoc, a mute goblin hauled before Mago on a charge of theft. The case was thin — suspicion dressed up as evidence — but what troubled the party first was simpler than that. Billoc's hands were bound. His only language was his hands. He stood accused in a court where he could not speak, and no one had thought to call this an injustice. Aldric, in his new capacity as Reader, called it exactly that. The party pressed the point with Mago: there was no proof, and a man who cannot defend himself has not had a defense. The goblin king was persuadable when the argument was sound, and the argument was sound. Billoc walked free, and the look he gave the party as his hands came loose carried more than gratitude — it carried the particular weight of someone who had not expected to be seen.

While Aldric stood in chambers, Magnolia made good on a promise she had carried since their first hours in the palace. The kitchens had been hospitable in their rough way, and she had offered something in return. She spent the spell slots she had been holding and produced fifty goodberries, which the head cook received with the reverence of someone who understood what the gesture cost and what the soup would mean. It was not a grand gesture by any court's reckoning, but it was a promise kept, and in the Shadow Fey lower court that counted for more than most forms of currency.

Tam took a different route entirely. He had noticed the stables on their first pass through the service level, and the Black Prince's hunting dogs had noticed him back. He returned with meat and the particular patience that wild things recognize as genuine, and by the time the dogs were eating from his hand the kennel master Kolya had warmed to him as well. From Kolya he learned the shape of the hunt — a high-court event, the quarry conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand from whatever summoner's art he practiced, and ridden with a great blue horse named Lazulin at the Black Prince's side. The hunt was a door, though what key it required remained unclear. Judith, meanwhile, tested the guard hall, where soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian occupied their cots and kept their swords sharp and revealed very little beyond their origins. The walls above the castle came up in the conversation, and the party noted the gap between where they stood and where the patrol routes ran.

By the time the day settled into whatever passed for evening in a realm that kept its own clock, the reckoning was plain enough: Aldric and Magnolia had climbed to a court status of seven, and Tam and Judith stood at five. The distance between them and the Summer Court above was still considerable, but the lower court had shown it could be moved through without blood, and the paths forward were beginning to suggest themselves. Billoc was somewhere in the palace now, free and in their debt, and Kolya knew Tam's name and what his hands could do with a dog that did not trust strangers. The hunt waited, and the season kept turning.
