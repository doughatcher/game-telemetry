The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from awkward. The guards ended the conversation satisfied. Judith ended it at status five.

That recovery is the thing worth noting. The guard hall encounter went badly before it went well, and the turn happened because Judith recognized the failure mid-performance, dropped the approach that wasn't working, and found a different angle without losing composure. A less adaptable person argues with the guard about the blood on the swords. Judith instead constructed a story about a platoon from Zobeck — satellite city, here to assist with the Shadow Fey's expansion — and delivered it with enough conviction to get a deception check that closed the gap. Improvisation under pressure is its own kind of skill, and she has it.
