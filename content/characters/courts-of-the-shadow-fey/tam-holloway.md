A human ranger with a Fey Wanderer's subclass and, somewhat at odds with that heritage, a deep and vocal resistance to the idea that any city should trade its freedom for the Fey Courts' version of order. That disposition has not softened inside the palace. When the party encountered soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian in the guard hall, Tam's read of the room was quick — he knows the shape of an occupying force when he sees one, and seeing it here, inside the court itself, does not seem to have made him more comfortable with any of this.

The stables were more his element. Tam found the Black Prince's hunting dogs with meat and animal speech — exactly the kind of approach that works when status and courtly maneuvering have their limits. Go to the creatures, speak their language, earn trust the direct way. Through Kolya the kennel master he learned that the Black Prince's hunt is a high-court event, that the quarry is conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand, and that the large blue horse named Lazulin belongs to the Black Prince himself. This is the kind of intelligence that does not come from formal inquiry — it comes from being the sort of person animals and kennel masters are willing to talk to.

Tam also probed the dueling culture of the lower court, establishing that victories raise rank and defeats cost it. He has not yet dueled. That he is asking the question suggests he is running calculations. Status five is respectable but not where he needs to be, and Tam strikes one as someone who would rather earn advancement through a clean fight than navigate the slower machinery of favors and impressions — even if the slower machinery is, at the moment, winning.
