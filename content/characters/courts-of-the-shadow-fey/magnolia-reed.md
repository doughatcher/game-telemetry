The only non-human in a party of humans, which has never seemed to particularly define her but does mean she notices things they don't. An elven druid of the Circle of Dreams — fey-adjacent by nature, which gives her a complicated relationship with the Shadow Fey occupation of Zobeck: less inclined toward outrage, more attuned to the particular grammar of how fey power operates. In the ambassador's negotiation, Magnolia positioned herself as the pragmatist — civilian safety over political outcomes, working within the power structure rather than against it. That instinct continues to serve her.

The kitchens are not where most adventurers look for political leverage. Magnolia made a promise to the kitchen staff earlier in the palace and kept it — fifty goodberries, delivered and crushed into soup by the head cook. In a court where status is currency, that act of follow-through bought something real. There is a version of this story where the promise gets quietly forgotten in favor of more obviously important business. Magnolia is not that version. A promise made to a cook matters the same as a promise made to a count, and the court has rewarded her accordingly — she sits at status seven, tied with Aldric for the highest in the party.

What distinguishes Magnolia from her companions is not power or cleverness but a kind of patient consistency. She does not chase status; she accumulates it by doing what she said she would do, by reading fey social structures the way another druid might read weather, and by treating the small obligations as seriously as the large ones. The Shadow Fey lower court is a place designed to humiliate and exhaust outsiders. Magnolia has thus far declined to be humiliated or exhausted.
