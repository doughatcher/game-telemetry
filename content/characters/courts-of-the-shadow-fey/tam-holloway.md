A human ranger with a Fey Wanderer's subclass and, somewhat at odds with that heritage, a deep and vocal resistance to the idea that any city should trade its freedom for the Fey Courts' version of order. That disposition has not softened inside the palace. When the party encountered soldiers from Allosis under Captain Memorethian in the guard hall, Tam's read of the room was quick — he knows the shape of an occupying force when he sees one, and seeing it here, inside the court itself, does not seem to have made him more comfortable with any of this.

The stables were more his element. Tam found the Black Prince's hunting dogs with meat and animal speech — exactly the kind of approach that works when status and courtly maneuvering have their limits. Go to the creatures, speak their language, earn trust the direct way. Through Kolya the kennel master he learned the name of the Black Prince's blue horse, Lazulin, and that the Prince's hunt is a high-court event drawing on quarry conjured by Count Samus Hollowgrand. This is the kind of intelligence that does not come from flattering courtiers. It comes from knowing which conversations to have in which rooms, and choosing the stable over the throne room when the stable is the right call.

Tam also tested the dueling culture directly, learning that rank can be won or lost on the field — a system he now understands well enough to be cautious with. He ended the session at status five, lower than Magnolia and Aldric but not for lack of effort. The court rewards certain kinds of performance. Tam is more interested in understanding the court than in performing for it, and that distinction may matter more as they move upward.
