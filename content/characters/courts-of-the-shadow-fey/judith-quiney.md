The name is doing a lot of work and it is doing all of it correctly. A human rogue who moves like someone who has thought carefully about when to use a blade and when to use a word, and who has arrived at the conclusion that both have their place and the choice is mostly about timing. When the Shadow Fey bard turned Magnolia's hand against the party, Judith killed the bard. That decision — fast, clean, correctly prioritized — says more about her than most conversations would.

In the Shadow Fey guard hall, Judith's instinct was to perform rather than persuade. Swords out, showing the soldiers what she could do, trying to establish credibility through visible competence. The first attempt landed poorly — shiny blades with no blood are not impressive to soldiers who have seen actual combat, and the guards said as much. She did not argue the point. She pivoted, found the thread of a deception instead, and pulled it hard enough to bring the whole encounter back from awkward. The guards ended the conversation with a better impression of the party than the opening had warranted. That recovery is its own kind of skill.

Status five is honest accounting for a session where the gains came from other directions, but Judith is not someone who measures herself purely by the court's ledger. She is learning the shape of this place — where blades impress and where they don't, where a well-constructed lie moves faster than the truth. The Shadow Fey court runs on performance. Judith has been performing since before she had the name to prove it.
