A human wizard who approaches the arcane with enough confidence to sometimes outpace his own caution. Divination gives him Portent — the ability to roll dice at dawn and slot predetermined results into moments that matter. This did not help him during the Shadow Road crossing, when a fireball of his own making briefly ended his participation in the journey. He was revived. His companions noted, with the restraint of people who have been through a lot together, that Portent applies to *others'* dice, not to consequences one has authored.

At the Shadow Fey palace, Aldric faced the accounting for that fireball directly. Summoned before King Mago over the destroyed wardstone, he did not deflect or apologize his way through it — he inspected the damage, understood what the stone actually was, and repaired it. Improved it. Ten-Pound Hammer accepted the work. This is the best possible outcome from a problem you caused yourself, and Aldric reached it through exactly the scholarly instinct that also causes the problems: look at the thing, understand it completely, then act.

King Mago did not let him off easily after that — he was pressed into service as Reader of the Lower Court, a role that requires legal standing and public judgment. The case of Billoc, a mute goblin accused of theft with his hands bound, was the first test. Aldric recognized the procedural injustice immediately: a man who cannot use his hands cannot speak in his own defense. He got the hands freed, the signing observed, and the case dismissed for lack of evidence. Billoc walked. That Aldric could move from repairing a wardstone to arguing fey law in the same afternoon suggests a mind that is genuinely at home in whatever system it finds itself inside — which is either an extraordinary gift or a quality that will eventually get him appointed to something he does not want.
